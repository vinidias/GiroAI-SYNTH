# Dataset card - GIRO-SYNTH

**Title:** GIRO-SYNTH - B2B Food Distribution with Known Behavioural Signals

**Subtitle:** 5,000 customers, 500 SKUs, 730 days, ~475k orders - with price
elasticity, churn and latent affinity generated from documented ground truth.

## Motivation

Existing synthetic B2B distribution datasets (including Atacado Atlas) are built
for data engineering: joins, FEFO, routes, finance. They do not simulate
behaviour, so predictive models cannot be meaningfully evaluated on them
(measured: elasticity R² 0.004, churn AUC 0.537).

GIRO-SYNTH fixes this by injecting three behavioural signals - each with a known
parameter - and publishing the ground truth alongside the operational tables.

## Contents

- 7 operational CSVs (customers, SKUs, price tables, salespeople, orders, items,
  monthly prices)
- 5 ground-truth CSVs (elasticity, churn, affinity, propensity, full demand panel)
- Manifesto, data dictionary, JSON schema, SHA-256 checksums
- Reproducible generator (seed + parameters)

## Provenance

Generated by a Python script; commodity prices come from CEPEA/ESALQ via
[agrobr](https://github.com/bruno-portfolio/agrobr). No real customer or company
data is included - names are synthetic.

## Recoverability

We publish measured results so users know the dataset works as advertised:

| Task | Metric | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Price elasticity | Pearson(true, estimated) | 0.995 |
| Churn | LightGBM ROC-AUC (temporal split) | 0.675 |
| Recommendation | ALS recall@10 | 0.246 |

## Intended uses

- Benchmarking causal/elasticity models
- Testing churn classifiers with correlated leading indicators
- Evaluating recommender systems with latent structure
- Teaching CRISP-DM with a dataset that has a real signal

## Limitations

- Synthetic: customer names, SKUs and routes are fabricated
- Elasticities are homogeneous within category by design
- Churn hazard is simplified (monthly Bernoulli from a fixed hazard)
- Commodity -> price coupling is a single pass-through coefficient
